About

Not just a dreamer — a builder with a P&L mindset.

I grew up inside a family business. Acropolis Mediterranean Grill isn't a line on a resume for me — it's six years of prep stations, peak-hour chaos, and being the person who gets called when the carbonation line fails or the POS system goes down mid-rush. I started as prep, worked my way to Assistant Kitchen Manager, and along the way picked up a second, unofficial job as the restaurant's facilities technician: gas fitting, soldering, electrical, plumbing — whatever kept the doors open and the tickets moving.

That environment taught me something before I ever opened a finance textbook: execution under pressure is a skill, not a personality trait. You learn it by being handed problems with no clean answer and a line of customers waiting.

"I'd rather be the person who reads the P&L and fixes the walk-in cooler than the person who only does one or the other."

That same instinct is what pulled me toward finance and entrepreneurship at the Haslam College of Business. I wanted the rigor — valuation, statistics, capital markets — paired with the ability to actually ship something. That combination is why I was selected as the student representative on Haslam's 15-member AI Task Force, presenting research directly to the Associate Dean and briefing high-profile alumni and donors on strategic AI adoption. It's also why I founded Apodexa instead of just writing a business plan about it.

Right now I'm building Apodexa, a research-first AI fitness and nutrition platform. The name comes from the Greek apódeixis — "proof" or "demonstration" — because the whole premise is that a recommendation isn't worth much unless it's demonstrable: sourced, cited, and defensible. The idea came from a gap I kept running into personally and kept hearing about from other serious athletes: most fitness apps are built on vibes, not literature. I wanted something grounded in a curated, allowlisted body of research — NIH, PubMed, ScienceDirect, the British Journal of Sports Medicine — with every recommendation traceable back to a cited source, not a black box.

I'm early. I know that. This portfolio is intentionally unfinished in places — there's a DCF model I'm still building, a venture financial model that's in progress, and a writing section that's just getting started. I'd rather show you the real state of the build than dress it up.

Apodexa — Research-First AI Fitness & Nutrition

A fitness and nutrition platform built for people who are serious about training, nutrition, and recovery — not a general wellness app. The core thesis: most fitness AI is confident but unsourced. Named for the Greek apódeixis ("proof, demonstration"), Apodexa grounds its guidance in a curated, allowlisted research corpus and cites it, numbered-footnote style, so users can see exactly where a recommendation comes from.

  • Identified the market gap and validated the concept with early customers and sales leads.
  • Scoped the product wedge: nutrient timing and periodization as the entry point, expanding into training, nutrition, and recovery reasoning.
  • Designed the freemium model — RAG retrieval free, frontier-model reasoning paid — to make the economics work at scale.
  • Built the research constitution as structured, metadata-tagged records (not a single blob of text) to support fast retrieval and clean citation.

Monte Carlo Risk Simulation

Built a 1,000-trial Monte Carlo simulation projecting Return on Assets for Palo Alto Networks (PANW) using five years of historical financials. Modeled Revenue, COGS, SG&A, D&A, Interest Expense, Non-Operating Income, and Tax Rate as random normal variables (via NORM.INV + RAND), then flowed each simulated income statement through to a resulting ROA.
